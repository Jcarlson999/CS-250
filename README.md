# CS 250 Portfolio Reflection

### How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I interpret user needs by understanding what users want to accomplish and what problems they need the program to solve. I then break those needs into smaller features with clear requirements. User stories help by explaining who needs a feature, what they need, and why it matters. Acceptance criteria give me a way to check whether the finished feature meets those expectations. This keeps development focused on the user instead of adding features without a clear purpose.

### How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach developing programs by breaking the work into manageable tasks, building one part at a time, and testing as I go. In future projects, I want to use a prioritized backlog and short sprints to stay organized and focus on the most important features first. Sprint reviews would help me gather feedback before moving too far in the wrong direction. Retrospectives would also help me identify what worked, what caused problems, and what I should change for the next sprint.

### What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member means being dependable, communicating clearly, and taking responsibility for assigned work. It also means speaking up when something is unclear or when a problem could affect the team’s progress. A good team member listens to feedback, respects other perspectives, and helps others when possible. My experience in the Army has shown me how much accountability and communication matter when people are working toward the same goal. Those same habits apply to software development.
